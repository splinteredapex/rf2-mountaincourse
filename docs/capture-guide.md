# Capturing drives with the phone logger

The pipeline stitches and averages any number of runs. They can be partial, overlap, and go in
either direction, on different days. More passes over a stretch means a better profile.

## What the phone can and cannot give

- **Can:** the height profile along the road (hills, crests, dips) from the barometer, levelled by GPS.
- **Cannot:** camber, road width or lane position. Phone GPS is only good to 3 to 5 m sideways.

## How to drive it

1. **Three passes or more over every stretch, in both directions.** The barometer lags slightly,
   which shifts a crest along the direction of travel. Opposite directions cancel that.
2. **Overlap partial runs by at least 500 m.** The overlap is what ties them together.
3. **Start recording, then wait 60 seconds before moving.** Do the same before stopping.
   GPS height needs time to settle and this gives the barometer a baseline.
4. **Windows closed, sunroof closed, fan on one constant setting.** Opening a window or changing
   the fan changes cabin pressure, which the barometer reads as a height change.
5. **Steady 30 to 40 mph where safe and legal.** Slower gives more samples per metre. Avoid hard
   acceleration and braking.
6. **Phone in a rigid mount, same place every run,** with a clear view of the sky (windscreen, not
   the glovebox or a pocket). Put the mount position in Run notes.
7. **Pick a settled day.** Avoid gusty wind and fast-changing weather; both move air pressure.
8. **Tap Mark point at landmarks** (bridge crests, junctions, village signs). I use them to check
   alignment.

## Pilot section

Union Mills to Crosby on the A1, about 5 km. Start a little before Braddan Bridge and finish a
little past Crosby so the ends are fully covered. Six passes (three each way) is a good target.

## Sending the runs

In the app: Recorded runs, Share, and send each .zip. Keep every run, including poor ones; the
pipeline rejects bad samples itself.
